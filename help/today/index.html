Your dashboard

What each tile and chart shows, and what "vs your average" means.

The first screen shows today, then the last 7 and 30 days. Every number is compared with your own recent history, never with other people.

All, your two sports, Other

Tabs at the top: All (every sport together, the default), your two sports, and Other for everything else. Choose them in Settings → First sport and Second sport; they start as Swimming and Running. A sport you didn't choose, such as cycling, is under Other.

All shows your last 7 days across every sport: total time against your usual week; a row of the last 7 days, today on the right (on the left in Arabic), with one symbol per sport you did that day and a ring for a day with no workout; and the cards that use every sport, such as Form, "Form over time" and "Training, last 7 days". A week counts as active if it has any workout.

Each sport tab has its own dashboard. Swims: when your Apple Watch records strokes, a pace is compared only with the same stroke, and mixed swimming and drills count in distance only. Runs: pace at your usual heart rate shows whether the same effort goes further; hills, heat and tiredness move it. Measurements such as sleep and resting heart rate have their own screen, the same for every sport.

Your sport first

A sport tab leads with that sport: your last 7 days of distance, time and pace, against your previous 4 weeks. Swims show minutes per 100 m (per 100 yd in the US), runs and walks minutes per kilometre (per mile in the US and UK) and rides speed in km/h (mph in the US and UK). With "other", you see time and the number of workouts, with no pace.

Always up to date with Apple Health

GSTS reads Apple Health when you open it, when you come back to it, and soon after your watch saves a workout. To read it now, pull down on the Overview. The line at the top says when: "Updated from Apple Health 3 min ago". All of it happens on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere.

The tiles

  • Exercise, steps, active energy: today's totals so far.
  • Resting heart rate: your heart rate at rest, measured by your watch. Lower than usual often goes with being well rested.
  • HRV (heart-rate variability): the small changes in time between heartbeats. Higher than usual often goes with being well rested. Values differ a lot between people: only your own trend matters.
  • Sleep: time asleep last night, with stages (core, deep, REM) if your watch records them.
  • Training load and form: see Training load and form.
  • This week: total time, and the time and distance of each sport, each in its own unit (metres for swims, kilometres for runs). Distances of different sports are never added together.

Bigger charts

A card opens only when its full view adds something. Tap it to open its full view: the same chart, bigger, with dates and units on its axes and the workouts behind it. Drag across it to read a value. You also see your average, your highest, lowest and latest values, and you can switch between 4 weeks, 12 weeks and 1 year. The free app shows 4 weeks; GSTS Pro shows the longer ranges.

Tap a bar or a point: one workout opens that workout, several open the Workouts list filtered to them. Back returns you to where you were.

What a card means

Tap the i at the top right of a card to read what it shows and how it's calculated. The full view has the same i in its top bar. GSTS describes your numbers; it never says good or bad.

Measurements in detail

Tap steps, sleep, resting heart rate or active energy to see a week, a month or a year. Tap a bar to read its value. Below the chart: your average, total, best and lowest, and the change against the period before. Today's bar is marked "so far" until the day ends. Turn the phone sideways for the chart at full screen.

On Android

Switch sport with one tap on Today; Settings holds only your default sport. Measurements have their own tab. Swims show each stroke and its pace only if the recording app saves them in Health Connect; otherwise distance leads, and pace covers all strokes, rests included. Android has no strokes per 100 m or efficiency map, because Health Connect has no stroke count. Ride leads on metres climbed if saved, otherwise distance. Exercise, steps and active energy tiles come later; today you see workouts, heart rate, resting heart rate, HRV, sleep, load and form. Android measures HRV in a different way from iPhone (RMSSD instead of SDNN), so the values aren't comparable between the two phones: compare only with your own history.

"vs your average"

Under a number you may see above, below or at your average. GSTS takes the same measure on the 28 days before today, skipping days without data, and checks how far today is from that average. It needs at least 7 days with data; until then it shows nothing.

"Above" isn't good or bad by itself: a lower resting heart rate is usually better, a higher HRV usually better. The tile shows which way is good.

When numbers are missing

A tile is empty when your phone has no data of that kind yet. See Troubleshooting.
